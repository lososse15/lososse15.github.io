Behav Neurol • 2026 • PMID: 42801787

Summary: This article explored oBJECTIVE: The aim of this study is to evaluate the effects of a unilateral-lower limb exoskeleton rehabilitation training robot on balance and lower limb function after stroke. METHODS: In this pilot study, stroke patients with hemiplegic at Tongji University Yangzhi Rehabilitation Hospital (between October 2022 and December 2023) were randomly assigned to either the robot group or the control group. Overall, the findings suggest no significant differences occurred in anxiety or pain VAS scores (p > 0.05). Key numbers reported in the abstract include: p < 0.001, p = 0.017, p = 0.030, p = 0.018, p = 0.046, p = 0.015, p > 0.05.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at helping older people stay steady and avoid falls. It suggests that stronger legs and better balance can keep people from tipping over. Practice can make walking and standing safer.

How to apply: Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe. Reinforce home safety and confidence-building repetitions. Emphasize task-specific gait practice at an appropriate challenge level (speed, distance, variability). Use cueing/feedback and progress intensity safely while monitoring fatigue and cardiovascular response.
