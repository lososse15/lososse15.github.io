The PROTECT-study (PROsthesis TElerehab Care Trial), home-based telerehabilitation in post-operative care after shoulder arthroplasty compared to usual care: study protocol for a randomized clinical trial

Trials • 2026 Aug 27 • PMID: 43058748

Summary: This article explored bACKGROUND: Total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) has become a commonly used procedure for patients with degenerative shoulder complaints such as severe osteoarthritis, cuff tear arthropathy and for revision surgery. After surgery, patients are generally referred to a physical therapist for rehabilitation. Overall, the findings suggest generally, the goals of rehabilitation are to increase the active range of motion, strengthen the shoulder and scapular muscles, and improve endurance.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at people with achy, stiff knees from arthritis. It suggests certain exercises can help the knee work better and hurt less. Moving and strengthening in the right way helps daily activities feel easier.

How to apply: Use progressive strengthening (quads/hips), functional training (sit-to-stand, stairs), and aerobic activity as tolerated. Track pain and function (e.g., LEFS/KOOS, sit-to-stand, gait speed), and progress load while keeping flare-ups manageable. Incorporate rotator cuff/scapular strengthening and progressive exposure to reaching/lifting based on irritability. Prioritize movement tolerance and function (reaching, overhead work), and progress dosage while monitoring next-day response. Focus on progressive lower-extremity strengthening, gait training, and functional tasks (sit-to-stand, stairs) with safety first. Progress assistive device use and endurance while tracking gait speed, sit-to-stand, and balance as appropriate.

Sports

Comparative effects of motor-control interventions on neuromuscular and biomechanical outcomes after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction: A systematic review and network meta-analysis

Complement Ther Med • 2026 Dec • PMID: 42946095

Summary: This article explored bACKGROUND: Neuromuscular-control and biomechanical deficits may persist after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction (ACLR). Comparative motor-control effects remain uncertain. Overall, the findings suggest proprioceptive training improved joint position sense versus usual rehabilitation (SMD 1.02, 95% CI 0.54-1.50; moderate CINeMA confidence); jump-landing control training yielded a favorable dynamic-balance estimate versus usual rehabilitation (1.19, 0.59-1.80; low CINeMA confidence) and was sensitive to exclusion of a late-phase multi-arm athlete trial; feedback-enhanced (0.88, 0.43-1.32) and proprioceptive training (0.59, 0.21-0.97) favored postural control (both low CINeMA confidence). Key numbers reported in the abstract include: P = 0.087, 95% CI 0.54-1.50.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at people recovering from an ACL injury. It suggests that getting the leg strong and controlled helps the knee stay stable. That means the right exercises can help people return to sports more safely.

How to apply: Use this information to strengthen the knee extensors/hip musculature and retrain landing/cutting mechanics. Progress from controlled strength work to plyometrics and sport-specific drills using criteria-based benchmarks (strength symmetry, hop/landing quality, workload tolerance). Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe. Reinforce home safety and confidence-building repetitions.

Geriatrics

Use of the Otago Exercise Programme for Fall Prevention in Adults After Stroke: A Best-Evidence Summary for Clinical Nursing Practice

J Nurs Scholarsh • 2026 Nov • PMID: 43016041

Summary: This article explored iNTRODUCTION: Falls are common after stroke, but evidence on the Otago Exercise Programme (OEP) specifically for post-stroke fall prevention remains limited. This evidence summary aimed to identify, appraise, and synthesize the best available evidence on the OEP for fall prevention in adults after stroke. Overall, the findings suggest rESULTS: In total, 14 publications were included, and 18 best-evidence statements were generated across 6 domains: fall risk assessment, exercise intervention principles, core OEP components, exercise prescription and implementation, safety management and health education, and intervention effects. Implementation should be guided by fall risk assessment, pre-exercise safety screening, individualized exercise prescription, patient and caregiver education, and continuity of care.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at helping older people stay steady and avoid falls. It suggests that stronger legs and better balance can keep people from tipping over. Practice can make walking and standing safer.

How to apply: Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe. Reinforce home safety and confidence-building repetitions. Emphasize task-specific gait practice at an appropriate challenge level (speed, distance, variability). Use cueing/feedback and progress intensity safely while monitoring fatigue and cardiovascular response.
