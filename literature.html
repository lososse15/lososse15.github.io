BMC Geriatr • 2026 Jul 31 • PMID: 42947012

Summary: This article explored bACKGROUND: Sarcopenia is an age-related condition characterized by gradual loss of muscle mass and strength, leading to reduced physical function, often observed among older adults. OBJECTIVE: This review aimed to evaluate the effects of resistance, aerobic, and balance exercises on muscle strength, functional ability, gait speed, balance, and quality of life (QoL) in adults with sarcopenia. Overall, the findings suggest the results of the analysis indicated that exercise interventions resulted in significant improvement of handgrip strength (standardized mean difference (SMD) = 0.39), knee extension strength (SMD = 0.62), gait speed (SMD = 0.66), and TUG (SMD = -0.73) compared with the control groups. Functional ability and QoL did not exhibit any significant differences between the groups.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at helping older people stay steady and avoid falls. It suggests that stronger legs and better balance can keep people from tipping over. Practice can make walking and standing safer.

How to apply: Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe. Reinforce home safety and confidence-building repetitions.
