Additional Effects of Manual Therapy to Exercise on Pain and Function in Individuals With Knee Osteoarthritis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Eur J Pain • 2026 Oct • PMID: 42885413

Summary: This article explored oBJECTIVE: This systematic review aimed to analyse the additional effects of manual therapy (MT) to exercise (EX) compared to EX alone on pain, function, and PPT in individuals with knee osteoarthritis (KOA). METHODS: Six databases (MEDLINE, PEDro, EMBASE, CENTRAL, CINAHL, and Web of Science) were searched from inception up to May 2026. Overall, the findings suggest standardized mean differences (SMD) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated by random-effects for meta-analyses. MT combined with EX, compared to EX alone, reduced pain in the immediate (mean difference [MD] = -0.53; 95% CI = -0.97 to -0.09 very low certainty of evidence), short-term (MD = -1.30; 95% CI = -1.74 to -0.87 very low certainty of evidence), and medium-term (MD = -0.98; 95% CI = -1.86 to -0.10 moderate certainty of evidence).

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at people with achy, stiff knees from arthritis. It suggests certain exercises can help the knee work better and hurt less. Moving and strengthening in the right way helps daily activities feel easier.

How to apply: Use progressive strengthening (quads/hips), functional training (sit-to-stand, stairs), and aerobic activity as tolerated. Track pain and function (e.g., LEFS/KOOS, sit-to-stand, gait speed), and progress load while keeping flare-ups manageable.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at helping older people stay steady and avoid falls. It suggests that stronger legs and better balance can keep people from tipping over. Practice can make walking and standing safer.

How to apply: Incorporate rotator cuff/scapular strengthening and progressive exposure to reaching/lifting based on irritability. Auditory Biofeedback Gait Training in Individuals With Chronic Ankle Instability: Protocol for a Randomized Controlled Trial

JMIR Res Protoc • 2026 Oct 22 • PMID: 42914108

Summary: This article explored bACKGROUND: Chronic ankle instability (CAI) develops in a large majority of patients with lateral ankle sprain (LAS), who often experience recurring injuries, residual feelings of instability, and abnormal neuromuscular patterns. Specifically, gait alterations are common in those with CAI and considered a contributing factor to poor long-term ankle joint health. Overall, the findings suggest oBJECTIVE: This study protocol introduces a progressive auditory biofeedback (ABF) gait-training program aimed to improve short- and long-term lower extremity biomechanics and talar cartilage outcomes and reduce the frequency of ankle-giving-way episodes and recurring injuries. Finally, the frequency of ankle-giving-way episodes in the 6 months prior to intervention will be compared to the frequency of episodes at the 6- and 12-month time points to determine a longitudinal difference in ankle-giving-way episodes. Key numbers reported in the abstract include: n=50, 12 months, 6 months.

Explain like I’m 5: This study tested a way to help people move better. It suggests the right kind of practice can improve strength or control. That can make everyday activities easier.

How to apply: Apply this by combining graded return-to-run programming with strength work (calf/hip/knee as indicated) and load management. Use symptom-guided progression, modify weekly volume, and address mechanics only when they meaningfully relate to symptoms.
