Virtual Reality-assisted Physiotherapeutic Training for Patients With Knee Osteoarthritis: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

Am J Phys Med Rehabil • 2026 Aug 1 • PMID: 42468005

Summary: This article explored oBJECTIVE: To evaluate the effectiveness of virtual reality (VR)-assisted physiotherapy in improving pain, physical function, and balance in patients with knee osteoarthritis (KOA) compared with traditional rehabilitation treatments. DESIGN: Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs). Overall, the findings suggest the pooled results showed that, compared with consistency rehabilitation, virtual reality (VR)-assisted physiotherapy produced significantly better outcomes in patients with knee osteoarthritis (KOA) for pain relief (SMD = 0.40, 95% CI: 0.14-0.65, P = 0.002), physical function (SMD = 0.37, 95% CI: 0.05-0.70, P = 0.003), and balance (SMD = 0.44, 95% CI: 0.16-0.72, P = 0.002). Key numbers reported in the abstract include: P = 0.002, P = 0.003, 95% CI: 0.14-0.65, 95% CI: 0.05-0.70, 95% CI: 0.16-0.72.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at people with achy, stiff knees from arthritis. It suggests certain exercises can help the knee work better and hurt less. Moving and strengthening in the right way helps daily activities feel easier.

How to apply: Use progressive strengthening (quads/hips), functional training (sit-to-stand, stairs), and aerobic activity as tolerated. Track pain and function (e.g., LEFS/KOOS, sit-to-stand, gait speed), and progress load while keeping flare-ups manageable. Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe. Reinforce home safety and confidence-building repetitions.
