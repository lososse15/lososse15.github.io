Extended Restitution Between Sessions Does Not Enhance the Benefits of 12 Weeks Exercise-Based Treatment for Patellar Tendinopathy: A Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial (The TEREX Trial)

Scand J Med Sci Sports • 2026 Mar • PMID: 41796988

Summary: This article explored loading intervention is the predominant treatment strategy for tendinopathy and the response may depend on restitution time between loading exposure. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to investigate if a 12-week exercise-based rehabilitation regime for patellar tendinopathy is influenced by restitution time. Overall, the findings suggest both groups attained significant improvements in clinical outcomes and muscular strength. In conclusion, there was no superior effect of 1 exercise day/week compared to 3 exercise days/week. Key numbers reported in the abstract include: 12 weeks.

Explain like I’m 5: This study tested a way to help people move better. It suggests the right kind of practice can improve strength or control. That can make everyday activities easier.

How to apply: Translate this into progressive tendon loading (isometrics → heavy slow resistance → plyometrics as tolerated). Monitor symptoms with a 24-hour response rule and gradually increase load/volume while maintaining movement quality. Apply this by combining graded return-to-run programming with strength work (calf/hip/knee as indicated) and load management. Use symptom-guided progression, modify weekly volume, and address mechanics only when they meaningfully relate to symptoms. Study-reported dosage (from the abstract): 12 weeks, 1 RM.
