Successful Conservative Treatment for Vancouver Type B3 Periprosthetic Femoral Fracture after Total Hip Arthroplasty: A Case Report

J Long Term Eff Med Implants • 2026 • PMID: 42870646

Summary: This article explored periprosthetic femoral fractures after total hip arthroplasty (THA) are increasing, though Vancouver type B3 fractures-characterized by a loose femoral stem and poor bone stock-are generally considered unsuitable for conservative treatment. We report a rare case of successful conservative management in a 79‑year‑old woman who sustained a Vancouver type B3 fracture 12 years after conversion of hip arthrodesis to THA. Overall, the findings suggest this case highlights that, although extremely rare, conservative treatment combined with sustained exercise therapy may achieve favorable outcomes in selected patients with Vancouver type B3 fractures when surgery is contraindicated. Key numbers reported in the abstract include: 20 months.

Explain like I’m 5: This study tested a way to help people move better. It suggests the right kind of practice can improve strength or control. That can make everyday activities easier.

How to apply: Focus on progressive lower-extremity strengthening, gait training, and functional tasks (sit-to-stand, stairs) with safety first. Progress assistive device use and endurance while tracking gait speed, sit-to-stand, and balance as appropriate.

Comprehensive Clinical Evaluation of the Combined Effects of Physical Therapy and Exercise Therapy in the Rehabilitation of Sports Injuries

J Vis Exp • 2026 Sep 11 • PMID: 42730692

Summary: This article explored sports injuries are common among athletes and recreationally active individuals, and rehabilitation outcomes influence functional recovery and the risk of re-injury. Physical therapy and exercise therapy are established rehabilitation strategies; however, evidence regarding their combined effectiveness remains limited. Overall, the findings suggest sports injuries are common among athletes and recreationally active individuals, and rehabilitation outcomes influence functional recovery and the risk of re-injury. Combined therapy was associated with greater improvements in pain, functional recovery, balance, and quality of life than physical therapy alone, with a comparable safety profile. Key numbers reported in the abstract include: n = 90, n = 95, P < 0.001, P = 0.001, P < 0.05, P > 0.05, 8 weeks.

Design and assessment of the feasibility of a diagnostic and rehabilitation device for gait re-education

Acta Bioeng Biomech • 2026 Jan 1 • PMID: 42885871

Summary: This article explored pURPOSE: Gait after a stroke is characterized by a number of abnormalities, such as reduced walking speed, impaired stability, as well as asymmetry. This study presents a novel assistive device designed to aid gait training in stroke survivors by providing integrated visual, auditory, and tactile stimuli informing about lower limb weight-bearing and step length symmetry. Overall, the findings suggest at the end of the program, decrease in step length asymmetry was observed and there was no statistically significant difference in the mean step length between the paretic and non-paretic lower limb (p = 0.002). Key numbers reported in the abstract include: p = 0.002, p = 0.0001.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at helping older people stay steady and avoid falls. It suggests that stronger legs and better balance can keep people from tipping over. Practice can make walking and standing safer.

How to apply: Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe. Reinforce home safety and confidence-building repetitions. Emphasize task-specific gait practice at an appropriate challenge level (speed, distance, variability). Use cueing/feedback and progress intensity safely while monitoring fatigue and cardiovascular response. Study-reported dosage (from the abstract): 30 min.
