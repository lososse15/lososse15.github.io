BMC Musculoskelet Disord • 2026 Aug 4 • PMID: 42977959

Summary: This article explored bACKGROUND: The prevalence of hip osteoarthritis is increasing and frequently requires total hip arthroplasty (THA). Overall, the findings suggest no statistically significant differences were found for the primary outcomes frontal knee, pelvis and trunk range-of-motion during level walking, nor the secondary outcomes Harris Hip Score (HHS), SF-12 and IPAQ short form.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at people with achy, stiff knees from arthritis. It suggests certain exercises can help the knee work better and hurt less. Moving and strengthening in the right way helps daily activities feel easier.

How to apply: Use progressive strengthening (quads/hips), functional training (sit-to-stand, stairs), and aerobic activity as tolerated. Track pain and function (e.g., LEFS/KOOS, sit-to-stand, gait speed), and progress load while keeping flare-ups manageable. Focus on progressive lower-extremity strengthening, gait training, and functional tasks (sit-to-stand, stairs) with safety first. Progress assistive device use and endurance while tracking gait speed, sit-to-stand, and balance as appropriate.

Effects of slow-belt split-belt treadmill training on balance, walking speed, and activities of daily living in patients with subacute stroke: A pilot randomized controlled trial

Medicine (Baltimore) • 2026 Nov 13 • PMID: 43024285

Summary: This article explored bACKGROUND: This study aimed to compare the effects of 3 weeks of slow-belt split-belt treadmill training (SBT) with those of tied-belt treadmill training (both belts driven at the same speed) on balance, functional reach, walking speed, and basic activities of daily living in patients with subacute stroke, and to evaluate the feasibility and safety of the SBT protocol. METHODS: In this single-center, assessor-blinded, parallel-group pilot randomized controlled trial with 1:1 allocation conducted in the inpatient rehabilitation unit of a tertiary hospital, 30 patients with subacute hemiplegic stroke (median, 13 days after onset) were randomized; 26 participants (13 per group) completed the post-intervention assessment. Overall, the findings suggest rESULTS: Both groups improved substantially (BBS, +18.8 vs +18.0 points; both P <.001); however, the baseline-adjusted between-group difference in the BBS was not significant (1.20 points; 95% confidence interval = -5.30 to 7.71; P =.706). No secondary outcome remained significant after Holm adjustment; a nominal difference in seated FRT (adjusted difference = 9.75 cm; P =.044; Holm-adjusted P =.355) was not robust in sensitivity analyses. Key numbers reported in the abstract include: 3 weeks, 13 days, 5 days.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at helping older people stay steady and avoid falls. It suggests that stronger legs and better balance can keep people from tipping over. Practice can make walking and standing safer.

How to apply: Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe. Reinforce home safety and confidence-building repetitions. Emphasize task-specific gait practice at an appropriate challenge level (speed, distance, variability). Use cueing/feedback and progress intensity safely while monitoring fatigue and cardiovascular response. Study-reported dosage (from the abstract): 3 weeks.
