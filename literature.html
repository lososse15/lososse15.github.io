Effect of short-term early postoperative gait-training using a lightweight hip-assist device on walking-speed recovery after total knee arthroplasty: a pilot study

J Artif Organs • 2026 Jun 16 • PMID: 42301523

Summary: This article explored robot-assisted gait training may improve walking recovery after total knee arthroplasty (TKA). However, optimal intervention durations and early postoperative feasibility remain unclear. Overall, the findings suggest significant time × group interactions were observed at 2 weeks for SWS (p = 0.045) and maximum walking speed (p = 0.044), indicating an attenuation of the early postoperative walking-speed decline in the HGT group. No significant group or interaction effects were observed for the range of motion, torque, or WOMAC outcomes. Key numbers reported in the abstract include: p = 0.045, p = 0.044, p = 0.013, 8 weeks, 2 weeks, 4 weeks.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at people with achy, stiff knees from arthritis. It suggests certain exercises can help the knee work better and hurt less. Moving and strengthening in the right way helps daily activities feel easier.

How to apply: Use progressive strengthening (quads/hips), functional training (sit-to-stand, stairs), and aerobic activity as tolerated. Track pain and function (e.g., LEFS/KOOS, sit-to-stand, gait speed), and progress load while keeping flare-ups manageable. Focus on progressive lower-extremity strengthening, gait training, and functional tasks (sit-to-stand, stairs) with safety first. Progress assistive device use and endurance while tracking gait speed, sit-to-stand, and balance as appropriate. Study-reported dosage (from the abstract): 20 min, 8 weeks, 2 weeks, 4 weeks.

Evaluating a 3-Month Physical Impairment and Functional Performance-Based Rehabilitation Program After Hip Arthroscopy for Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI) Syndrome

J Orthop Sports Phys Ther • 2026 Jul • PMID: 42381614

Summary: This article explored oBJECTIVE: To evaluate a 3-month post-hip arthroscopy rehabilitation program within a randomized controlled trial comparing hip arthroscopy to sham surgery for femoroacetabular impingement syndrome (HIP ARThroscopy International [HIPARTI] trial) by describing exercise adherence, type, and pain; 6-month changes in International Hip Outcome Tool-33 (iHOT-33); and physical impairments and functional performance. DESIGN: Exploratory cohort study nested in a pilot randomized controlled trial. Overall, the findings suggest hip extension, adduction, external and internal rotation strength improvements exceeded the minimal detectable change, although they were not significant.

Explain like I’m 5: This study tested a way to help people move better. It suggests the right kind of practice can improve strength or control. That can make everyday activities easier.

How to apply: Integrate the intervention into a criteria-based sport progression: restore strength symmetry, neuromuscular control, and graded exposure to sport demands with objective testing where possible. Study-reported dosage (from the abstract): 2 sessions per week.

Summary: This article explored oBJECTIVES: To compare the effects of a 4-week dynamic, multidirectional therapeutic exercise (TEx) programme alone versus TEx combined with neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES) in individuals with chronic ankle instability (CAI). DESIGN: Randomised intervention study. Overall, the findings suggest cONCLUSIONS: NMES combined with TEx may improve the timing of neuromuscular activation during inversion-simulation tasks, while TEx showed within-group improvements in some dynamic balance outcomes.
