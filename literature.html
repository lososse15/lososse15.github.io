Comment on "swedish massage versus hip strengthening exercises for pain and function in older adults with knee osteoarthritis: a randomized controlled trial"

Aging Clin Exp Res • 2026 Jul 30 • PMID: 42530720

Summary: This article explored the results of the randomised controlled trial by Asgarimoghadam et al. (2026) on the use of Swedish massage and hip-strengthening exercises in older adults with knee osteoarthritis are quite informative regarding non-pharmacological management strategies. Overall, the findings suggest the lack of participant blinding with the use of subjective outcome measures is a potential risk of performance bias. Also, the sample size used is not very large, which raises doubts about the lack of statistical power to identify the differences between two active interventions contributing to an increased risk of a Type II error.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at people with achy, stiff knees from arthritis. It suggests certain exercises can help the knee work better and hurt less. Moving and strengthening in the right way helps daily activities feel easier.

How to apply: Use progressive strengthening (quads/hips), functional training (sit-to-stand, stairs), and aerobic activity as tolerated. Track pain and function (e.g., LEFS/KOOS, sit-to-stand, gait speed), and progress load while keeping flare-ups manageable.

Summary: This article explored bACKGROUND: First rib fractures are infrequently encountered injuries, especially in the young athlete. Overall, the findings suggest mean time to presentation was 128 days (3 to 450 d) for patients without a history of trauma, compared with 21.9 days (0 to 96 d) for those with a history of traumatic injury, P =0.127. Design and validation of an immersive virtual rehabilitation system for individuals with vestibular disorders: A feasibility study

PLoS One • 2026 • PMID: 42531289

This can reduce adherence and limit treatment outcomes. Overall, the findings suggest this can reduce adherence and limit treatment outcomes. Key numbers reported in the abstract include: p = 0.002, p = 0.007, p = 0.012, p = 0.081.

Explain like I’m 5: This study looked at helping older people stay steady and avoid falls. It suggests that stronger legs and better balance can keep people from tipping over. Practice can make walking and standing safer.

How to apply: Use multicomponent fall-prevention programming: strength + balance + gait tasks, progressing from stable to dynamic environments and adding dual-task when safe.
